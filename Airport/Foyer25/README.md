# Entrance foyer — room-one review

This revision focuses on the entrance foyer, a 6 × 10 m room with a 3.6 m ceiling, and its short screened transitions. Check-in now sits around a turn in a recessed approach; a corridor return hides security equipment from the foyer. The rest of the airport and parked aircraft are retained from Rooms24 for continuity. Their art is not approved by this room review.

Run **Run Foyer25.cmd** for the editable Unreal preview. The installed Unreal Engine is required; this is not a packaged standalone executable. WASD/mouse moves and looks; E/click uses the aimed item; Space stands from a seat; Tab toggles spectator. The information call button, two waiting seats, suitcase and extinguisher are usable. The suitcase starts stowed upright on its rack and becomes a simulated rigid body on pickup; it remains simulated after dropping.

The entrance opening is 2.7 m wide, the offset check-in entrance has 1.90 m clear width and a 1.88 m deep pocket, and the north corridor opening retains its 3 m width. The corridor screen sits 2 m beyond that threshold, with the onward route around its east end. Furniture stays in the perimeter bays. Waiting chairs have 550 mm wide contoured upholstery, a nominal 460 mm seat height and 760 mm spacing. The check-in sign has 3.06 m clearance beneath its housing. These are authored design dimensions, not a certification against building or accessibility regulations.

`Airport_Foyer25.blend` contains the editable placed airport and the new foyer asset library. `Exports/airport_layout.json` is the matching native layout; `FoyerChanges25.json` records the exact removed/relocated items. Eight exported mesh families cover the new floor/mat, room envelope, custom two-seat bench/rack, closed information cabinet/screen, mounted signs/clock/lights, screened approaches/destination artwork, welcoming entrance/seating finish and retained fixtures for other rooms. Upholstery uses a continuous contoured shell, not separate box cushions. The floor uses one aligned 600 mm tile grid around a flush framed ribbed mat. Ceiling baffles and lights connect to the ceiling, the display attaches to its backing, and the cabinet has closed sides/back, fitted doors and toe space. One check-in kiosk moves clear of the recessed approach; its interaction and physical proxy move with it. The information host moves into the side bay, with its runtime placement and body proxy kept in agreement.

The welcome pass carries timber beyond the original corner: a two-faced framed entrance, an acoustic canopy over the entrance mat, restrained vertical practical lighting, navy interior glazing panels with timber caps, a sage acoustic seating backdrop, a round oak side table and layered travel guides. The room uses warmer, deeper stone and wall colours. The source's existing clean window materials are retained. The native meshes for the current assembly live under `/Game/Airport25/MeshesPrivacy`, keeping material-slot order isolated from older mesh versions.

Rebuild with Blender `--background --disable-autoexec --python SourceAssets/model_foyer25.py`, then the native Unreal Python commandlet on `SourceAssets/build_foyer25_unreal.py`, or use Python `SourceAssets/rebuild25.py` for that serialized pipeline. This always starts from the preserved Rooms24 source/map. Run Python `SourceAssets/run25.py qa` for the bounded foyer test and `SourceAssets/run25.py photos` for eight native camera views (seven room views and one entrance approach), then `SourceAssets/make_review25.py` to verify hashes and regenerate the review. Heavy engine helpers should run serially.

The first engine images exposed an occluded departures display and a tipped suitcase. They are retained under `ArtBible/Diagnostics/FirstEngineViews25`; the direction before the user's privacy refinement is preserved under `BeforePrivacy25`, including native assets and editable source. Final images are under `Screenshots`. The final reports record the actual result. QA is scoped to this room's routes, sightline screening, aiming, furniture interactions and eye height. It is not a new full-airport playthrough or a frame-rate certification. Character art, exterior landscaping and the still-white next room are separate review tasks.
